# Foundational Assumptions

## Purpose
State the philosophical and architectural assumptions Cortex depends on so future changes can be tested against first principles.

## Assumptions About Organizational Reality
- Organizations produce reconstructable digital exhaust across tools.
- Organizational behavior can be partially represented as events, entities, relations, and temporal transitions.
- Decision and execution history is recoverable with sufficient provenance continuity.

## Assumptions About Modeling
- Canonicalization can normalize cross-tool semantics enough to support shared ontology.
- Temporal reconstruction is achievable using event-time anchors and validity windows.
- Causality cannot be proven exhaustively from operational data, but useful hypotheses can be generated with explicit uncertainty.

## Assumptions About Infrastructure
- Deterministic infrastructure should dominate ambiguous inference pathways.
- Replayability is mandatory because schemas, mappings, and inference models will evolve.
- Raw immutability is required to preserve trust in reconstruction and reprocessing.

## Assumptions About AI
- AI is useful for ambiguity resolution and synthesis, not for foundational truth creation.
- AI outputs are inherently non-authoritative without evidence and confidence semantics.
- AI behavior must remain bounded, inspectable, versioned, and replay-comparable.

## Assumptions About Governance
- Architecture drift is inevitable without explicit invariants and escalation rules.
- Contract and ontology stability must precede implementation.
- Traceability and review discipline are required to keep a multi-year cognition system coherent.
